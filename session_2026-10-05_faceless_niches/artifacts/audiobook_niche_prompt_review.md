## Line-by-Line Breakdown — Why Each Part Exists

### Opening line
> *"I'm evaluating the YouTube audiobook/book summary niche before starting a channel."*

Tells the AI exactly what stage you're at — pre-launch research. Without this, it might give you growth tips instead of viability data.

> *"No fluff, no motivation, just facts and risks."*

Forces the AI to skip the "Great question! Here's why this niche is amazing..." sycophancy. Without this, you'll get 40% filler.

---

### MY PLAN section
> *"AI usage: ~90%"*

Fixed the 90/95 contradiction. 90% is accurate — you're doing script QC manually, that's human editorial input.

> *"I only do manual script quality check and editing"*

This is critical context. It tells the AI your exact level of human involvement, which directly affects the monetization risk answer.

---

### Question 1 — VIABILITY
> *"Give me realistic monthly search volume and competition level for keywords"*

This is vidIQ's core strength — keyword data. By listing specific keywords, you prevent the AI from giving vague answers like "it's a growing niche."

> *"check both Hindi and English"*

Forces a direct comparison instead of a generic answer.

---

### Question 2 — MONETIZATION RISK
> *"Are AI audiobook channels actually getting monetized right now?"*

This is the real question. Not "can they be monetized theoretically" but "are they monetized TODAY."

> *"I have the NexLev extension so I can verify any channel's monetization status myself — give me specific channels to check."*

This is key. Instead of the AI claiming "yes these channels are monetized" (which could be hallucinated), you're telling it to give you names so YOU can verify with NexLev. Keeps the AI honest.

---

### Question 3 — RPM & REVENUE
> *"What's the difference between Hindi and English RPM in this specific niche?"*

"In this specific niche" is important. Generic Hindi vs English RPM data is useless — audiobook RPMs are different from gaming RPMs. Forces niche-specific data.

---

### Question 4 — COPYRIGHT
> *"Have any book summary channels been struck or taken down"*

Reframed from "what happens if I summarize copyrighted books" (legal question vidIQ can't answer) to "have channels been struck" (observable fact vidIQ CAN answer).

> *"full narration vs summary vs commentary"*

These are the three formats with very different risk levels. Full narration of a copyrighted book = high risk. Summary with commentary = transformative use = lower risk. This forces the AI to distinguish.

---

### Question 5 — HINDI vs ENGLISH
> *"Consider: competition density, audience size, RPM, and number of existing channels"*

Without these specific criteria, the AI will just say "English has more audience" and stop. By listing 4 factors, you force a multi-dimensional answer.

---

### Question 6 — TOP CHANNELS
> *"Give me the top 5 Hindi and top 5 English audiobook/book summary channels right now (outside of my list below)"*

Your given channels are the ones that motivated you — but they might be outliers. By asking for the TOP channels outside your list, you see the full competitive landscape. If the top channels are massive (1M+ subs, consistent views), the niche is proven. If even the top channels are struggling, it's a red flag.

> *"I need this to judge if this niche is actually worth entering"*

Tells the AI WHY you're asking — so it picks channels that actually represent niche health, not random small ones.

---

### Question 7 — FORMAT REPLICABILITY
> *"Look at @EnglishAvenuee — this channel uses a simple format: real human voiceover + captions on whiteboard/background images"*

This is your key insight. EnglishAvenuee proves that minimal production can earn big IF the format is right. The channel is human-made, not AI — but the format is so simple that AI could replicate it.

> *"Can I replicate this exact simple format using AI tools and still get monetized without being flagged?"*

This is the real question you're trying to answer with this entire research. Everything else (keywords, RPM, copyright) is secondary to this: "Can I build a channel this simple with AI and make money?"

---

### Question 8 — OTHER NICHES
> *"What are 5-10 other YouTube niches where this same low-effort format works"*

This is your escape hatch. If audiobook summaries turn out to be too risky or saturated, you need backup niches that use the SAME production pipeline (AI voice + captions + simple images). By asking for 5-10 alternatives, you're not locked into one niche.

> *"AI voiceover + simple visuals (captions over images, whiteboard style, or static backgrounds) + minimal editing = good earnings"*

This description is extremely specific about what "simple format" means. Without it, the AI might suggest niches that need fancy 3D animation or face-on-camera — useless to you.

> *"I want niches where the content itself carries the value (not fancy visuals)"*

This is the key filter. In audiobooks and educational content, people listen — they don't watch. The audio IS the product. This line forces the AI to only suggest niches where that's true.

---

### CHANNELS TO ANALYZE
> *"@SoulArcStudios, @LifeUpgrade27..."*

Using @ handles instead of full URLs — vidIQ AI Coach recognizes handles better and can pull channel data directly.

> *"@EnglishAvenuee — this is NOT AI-generated. It's human voiceover with simple captions on background images."*

Corrects the AI's assumption before it happens. Without this clarification, vidIQ will assume it's AI content and give you a wrong risk analysis. By telling it upfront "this is human-made but looks simple," you get an honest answer about whether AI replication of this format would survive.

> *"For each channel: subscribers, total views, upload frequency, average views per video, and estimated RPM range."*

Without this line, the AI will give you vague qualitative descriptions. This forces a data table.
