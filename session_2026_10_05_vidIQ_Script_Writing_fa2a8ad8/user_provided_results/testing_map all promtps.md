## STEP A: One-Shot Prompt (10 credits)

> Attach Video 1 via "Add from YouTube" first, then paste this.

```
I'm making a 15-minute faceless YouTube video on how ancient humans survived the Ice Age. I've attached a high-performing reference video on the same topic. I want you to study HOW that video works — its pacing, hooks, transitions, retention tricks — and then write me an ORIGINAL script that uses the same techniques but different content and wording.

Here's exactly what I need in this one response:

1. ANALYZE the reference video's structure: how many sections it has, how the hook works, where it places re-hooks, how it transitions between topics, what storytelling techniques it uses to keep viewers watching.

2. MAP a new script structure using those techniques. Do NOT copy the reference's section order. Rearrange topics for maximum curiosity.

3. WRITE the full narration script (~2,200-2,400 words, 15-minute read time).

RULES — READ EVERY SINGLE ONE:

WHAT YOU MUST NOT DO:
- Do NOT paraphrase or closely rewrite the reference video's hook, sentences, or wording. Zero copied sentences. I will check.
- Do NOT start with the same "you're warm right now" hook approach the reference uses. Use a completely different hook angle — maybe start with a specific survival scenario, a death statistic, or a mystery.
- Do NOT use the same section order as the reference. If the reference goes Fire → Clothing → Shelter, you go in a different order.
- Do NOT add a separate "Introduction" section after the hook. Go directly from hook into the first content section. No momentum dump.
- Do NOT end with "hit like and subscribe" or any generic YouTube CTA. End with a thought-provoking closer that makes the viewer sit with it.
- Do NOT write like a Wikipedia article or documentary textbook. No sentences like "The Great Ice Age wasn't just one long winter. It was a chaotic mess of glaciers." That is boring.

WHAT YOU MUST DO:
- The narrator has personality. Think: a smart friend who just read 10 books on this and can't wait to tell you the craziest parts. Talks TO the viewer, not AT them. Example of GOOD narration: "Here's a wild fact most people miss." Example of BAD narration: "It is important to note that early humans developed sophisticated tools."
- Each section follows this pattern: present the brutal problem first (make the viewer feel how deadly it was) → reveal the insane method they invented (make the viewer think "wait, really?") → explain why this changed everything for humanity.
- Include mid-video re-hooks. At roughly the 7-8 minute mark, drop a line like "Now here's where it gets strange" or "But none of that compares to what they did next" to recapture anyone drifting.
- Include at least one humor beat or tonal shift. Break the serious pattern at least once. It can be dry humor, a modern comparison, or a "imagine trying that today" moment.
- Every section must have at least one specific, named detail — a site name, a date, a study, an archaeological finding. No vague "ancient humans were clever" filler.

MANDATORY CONTENT — These must appear in the script (the reference video covers some of these, but the script I got before missed all of them):
- The hibernation theory from the Pit of Bones in Spain (~430,000 years ago) — cyclical bone damage suggesting seasonal dormancy
- Bone sunglasses — carved slits in bone/wood to prevent snow blindness
- Cold trap doorway design — entrance dug lower than sleeping area so cold air sinks
- Tallow (animal fat) rubbed on skin as insulation before hunts
- The Yaghan people of South America — ran hotter body temperatures, survived freezing conditions with almost no clothing
- The eyed bone needle (~30,000 years ago) as the game-changing invention for fitted clothing
- Mammoth bone houses at Mezhyrich, Ukraine

FORMAT: Pure narration script. No stage directions, no "[B-roll of glacier]", no visual notes. Just the words the narrator speaks. Faceless channel — no "hey guys", no face references, no "as you can see."
```

## STEP B: Multi-Step Prompts (Use ONLY if one-shot fails)

### Multi-Step Prompt 1 — Pure Analysis, No Writing (10 credits)

```
Forget the script you just wrote. Let's start fresh but stay in this conversation.

I need you to do ONE thing only: forensic analysis of the reference video I attached.

Break down exactly:
1. How does the hook work? What technique does it use to grab attention in the first 15 seconds?
2. What is the exact section structure? List every topic section, roughly how long each one is, and what order they come in.
3. Where are the re-hooks? At what points does the narrator recapture attention mid-video?
4. What retention techniques does it use? (Open loops, mystery, contrast, direct address, humor, tonal shifts)
5. How does it transition between sections? Does it use cliffhangers, questions, or smooth bridges?
6. What makes the narrator's voice distinctive? What specific word choices or sentence patterns give it personality?

Give me ONLY the analysis. Do NOT write any script yet. I need to see your understanding before we write anything.
```

### Multi-Step Prompt 2 — Structure + First Half (10 credits)

```
Good analysis. Now two things:

FIRST: Create a new script skeleton for "Ice Age Survival: The Brutal Inventions That Saved Humanity" (15 min). Use the reference's RETENTION TECHNIQUES from your analysis, but:
- Different section order than the reference
- Different hook approach entirely
- Must include these topics: fire mastery, clothing/eyed needle, mammoth bone shelters, stone toolkit, hunting + food preservation, cooperation/society
- Must ALSO include: hibernation theory (Pit of Bones, Spain), bone sunglasses, cold trap doorway design, tallow skin insulation, Yaghan people adaptation
- Place a re-hook at the midpoint
- Include one humor beat somewhere in the second half

Show me the skeleton with section titles and 1-line descriptions.

SECOND: Write the first half of the script — from the Hook through the midpoint (~1,100-1,200 words). The hook must NOT use the "you're warm right now" approach. The narrator talks like a smart friend who's excited about the topic, not a textbook. Every section needs at least one named site, date, or study. End the first half on an open loop.
```

### Multi-Step Prompt 3 — Second Half (10 credits)

```
Continue from where you left off. Write the second half of the script (~1,100-1,200 words).

Rules for this half:
- Start with a re-hook that recaptures attention — a tonal shift or a "now here's the part nobody talks about" moment
- The hibernation theory from the Pit of Bones MUST be in this half — present it as a genuine unsettled mystery
- Include the humor beat here — a modern comparison, a "imagine doing this today" moment, or dry wit. Break the serious pattern
- The Yaghan people section should feel like a "plot twist" — everything we've talked about was inventing solutions, but these people's bodies actually BECAME the solution
- The conclusion must NOT be a generic CTA. End with a thought that connects ancient survival to something the viewer feels today. Make them sit with it. No "hit like and subscribe" — either skip it entirely or weave engagement into the closing naturally
- Keep the same narrator voice from the first half — personality, direct address, conversational
```
