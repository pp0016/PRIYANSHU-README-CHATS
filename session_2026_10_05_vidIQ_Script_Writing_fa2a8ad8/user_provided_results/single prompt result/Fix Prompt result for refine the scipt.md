Here's a detour worth taking, because some people skipped all the engineering entirely. At the southern tip of South America — a cold, sodden corner called Tierra del Fuego — the Yaghan people lived in a place so brutal they wore next to nothing. When Charles Darwin visited in the eighteen thirties, he wrote that he could barely believe his eyes: people standing nearly naked in freezing sleet and snow, and somehow looking comfortable. The trick wasn't what they wore. Their bodies measured consistently hotter than a typical human's, generating more heat from the inside. So they could sit on a freezing beach in a wind that would break someone else in half, and they'd rest in a deep squat, tucking their arms and legs in against their core to shrink the surface their body had to warm. When the answer to the cold isn't technology, sometimes it's your own metabolism turned up to a setting you can't buy.
