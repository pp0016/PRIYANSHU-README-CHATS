# FACT-CHECK PASS — standalone

**Use:** paste the whole thing below into a **brand-new conversation** (vidIQ AI Coach on a fresh
Gmail, a new Antigravity session, or Claude). Paste your script under it. Never run it in the
conversation that wrote the script.

**Why never the same conversation:** on 28 Sep the agent that designed the prompts also graded
the output and gave itself 9.5/10 on a script whose timestamps were fiction and which cited a
YouTube video as an archaeological source. An author cannot find its own blind spot.

---

## THE CORE PROBLEM THIS SOLVES

AI models invent citations that look perfect. A fabricated source usually has a real journal name,
a plausible author, a believable year, and a DOI that does not resolve. You cannot spot this by
reading — the fake looks exactly like the real one.

**The mechanism that defeats it:** force the model to quote the exact sentence from the source
that supports the claim. A model cannot quote a paper it never read. If it will not quote, it does
not have the source.

---

## SOURCE TIERS — teach this to the model every time

**TIER 1 — PRIMARY.** The thing itself.
Court filings, SEC filings, patents, government inquiry reports, accident investigation reports,
company annual reports, original photographs, museum catalogue entries, contemporaneous newspaper
reporting, the original text of a document.

**TIER 2 — PEER-REVIEWED.** Named journal, named authors, year, DOI.
*Nature, Science, PNAS, Journal of Human Evolution, Antiquity, PLOS One, Journal of Archaeological
Science.* A preprint (arXiv, bioRxiv) is Tier 2 only if labelled as a preprint in the script.

**TIER 3 — INSTITUTIONAL.** A body with a reputation to lose.
NASA, ESA, NIST, NTSB, Natural History Museum, Smithsonian, British Museum, Canadian Museum of
History, national archives, university departments, ASCE, Institution of Civil Engineers.

**TIER 4 — QUALITY JOURNALISM WITH NAMED REPORTING.**
Reuters, AP, FT, WSJ, NYT, BBC, Nikkei — only where the piece names its own sources. Usable for
business/recent-events claims. Not usable for scientific or archaeological claims.

### BANNED — never a source, no exceptions
- **Another YouTube video, including your reference video.** This is the error that got into your
  Ice Age script: 600°C and the 300,000-year bear paws were cited to `wt_7Hp7d7I8`.
- Anything generated by an AI, including this model's own memory with no citation.
- Content farms, SEO listicles, "10 amazing facts" sites, Medium posts, Quora, Reddit.
- Pinterest, Facebook posts, TikTok.
- **Wikipedia as a terminal source.** Wikipedia is a *finding aid* — go to its footnotes and cite
  what IT cites. If a Wikipedia footnote is dead or missing, the claim is unverified.

### Where to actually look, by niche

| Niche | Go here first |
|---|---|
| Business history / rise & fall | **SEC EDGAR** (10-K, 8-K, S-1 — free, full text, searchable), UK Companies House, PACER for litigation, the company's own annual reports on archive.org Wayback, Harvard Business School case studies, FT/WSJ archives |
| Engineering / megaproject failure | **NTSB** accident reports, **NIST** investigation reports, ASCE *Journal of Structural Engineering*, Institution of Civil Engineers proceedings, government commission reports, Engineering News-Record |
| History / inventions | Google Scholar, JSTOR, national archives (UK National Archives, US NARA), university press books, Internet Archive full-text search, newspapers.com |
| Archaeology / prehistory | *Journal of Human Evolution*, *Antiquity*, *Journal of Archaeological Science*, *PLOS One*, *Nature*, *Science*, plus the excavating institution's own site reports |
| Space / astronomy | **NASA ADS** (adsabs.harvard.edu), arXiv, NASA and ESA mission pages, *ApJ*, *MNRAS* |
| Religion / apocrypha | Primary translations (e.g. R.H. Charles 1917 for 1 Enoch — public domain), Oxford/Cambridge academic commentary, the Dead Sea Scrolls digital library |
| Book summaries | The book itself. Page numbers. Nothing else needed. |

## THE FIVE FAILURE CLASSES

These are the specific ways scripts die in the comments. Named from real errors found in your
Ice Age script.

**1. DERIVED PRECISION — the #1 killer.**
A range restated as a single figure. Your script said Mezhyrich was *"in use for as long as 429
years"*, derived from a date range of 18,250–17,750 years ago. Radiocarbon gives a range of
*when*, never a duration of *how long*. The whole line "nobody rebuilds a bad idea for four
hundred years" rested on it.
→ Check every duration, every "for X years", every average, every percentage.

**2. LAUNDERED SOURCE.** Cited to a YouTube video, blog, or content farm and then stated with
academic confidence.

**3. CONTESTED AS SETTLED.** A minority or disputed position presented as fact. Your script did
this with Yaghan elevated metabolic rate, which is contested and confounded by diet.

**4. INTERNAL INCONSISTENCY.** The same fact given two values in one document. Your script dated
Volgu as 22,000–18,000 in the skeleton and 21,000–18,000 in the proof list.

**5. OUT-OF-SCOPE EVIDENCE.** Evidence from the wrong period, region, or field used to support
a claim about this one. Your script used Inuit snow goggles — Arctic, thousands of years later —
as evidence about European Ice Age technology, and admitted it inside the script.

## THE PROMPT — copy everything below this line

```
You are fact-checking a script you did not write. You are adversarial. Do not compliment it.
Do not soften anything. Your job is to find what is wrong.

=== SOURCE RULES YOU MUST FOLLOW ===

For EVERY source you give me, you must provide all five of these:
  1. Exact title
  2. Author(s)
  3. Year
  4. Publication or institution
  5. A URL or DOI I can open

AND — this is the part that matters most —
  6. THE EXACT SENTENCE from that source that supports the claim, in quotation marks.

If you cannot quote the supporting sentence, you do not have the source. In that case write
UNVERIFIED — I could not access a source for this. Do not guess. Do not reconstruct a citation
from memory. A confident wrong citation is worse for me than an admitted gap, because I will
publish it.

SOURCE TIERS — rank every source you give me:
  TIER 1 PRIMARY: court filings, SEC filings, patents, government/accident inquiry reports,
    company annual reports, original documents, contemporaneous newspaper reporting
  TIER 2 PEER-REVIEWED: named journal + authors + year + DOI
  TIER 3 INSTITUTIONAL: NASA, NIST, NTSB, national museums, national archives, universities
  TIER 4 JOURNALISM: Reuters, AP, FT, WSJ, NYT, BBC — business and recent events only,
    never for scientific or archaeological claims

BANNED as sources, with no exceptions:
  - ANY YouTube video, including the reference video this script was based on
  - Anything AI-generated, including your own memory without a citation
  - Content farms, SEO listicles, Medium, Quora, Reddit, Pinterest
  - Wikipedia as a final source. Wikipedia is a finding aid only: go to its footnotes and cite
    what IT cites. Dead or missing footnote = UNVERIFIED.

=== PASS A: EXTRACT EVERY CLAIM ===

Pull every factual claim — number, date, place, named person, study, quantity, superlative —
into a table:

| # | Claim (quoted from script) | Source the script gave | Tier | Verdict | Real source + quoted sentence |

Verdict is one of: CONFIRMED / WRONG / UNVERIFIED / OVERSTATED.

=== PASS B: THE FIVE FAILURE CLASSES ===

Check every claim against these five and flag by name:

1. DERIVED PRECISION — a range turned into a single figure. Check EVERY duration, "for X years",
   average, and percentage. Example of the error: a carbon date of "18,250-17,750 years ago"
   written up as "in use for 429 years". A date range is not a duration. This is the most common
   and most damaging error class — check it first and check it hardest.
2. LAUNDERED SOURCE — cited to a video, blog, or content farm, then stated with authority.
3. CONTESTED AS SETTLED — disputed or minority position presented as established fact.
4. INTERNAL INCONSISTENCY — the same fact given two different values anywhere in the script.
5. OUT-OF-SCOPE EVIDENCE — wrong period, wrong region, or wrong field for the claim it supports.

For each flag give me: the exact fix, or "CUT THIS LINE".

=== PASS C: WHAT I CAN SPOT-CHECK ===

Give me THREE claims you are least confident about, with their URLs, so I can open them myself
and confirm you are not inventing sources. Pick the three where being wrong would hurt most.

=== OUTPUT ===

1. The claim table.
2. A numbered FIX LIST — every line that must change before recording, in script order, with the
   replacement text written out so I can paste it.
3. A CUT LIST — claims with no findable source. Say plainly that these must be removed.
4. One line: how many claims were CONFIRMED out of the total.

Do not rewrite the script. Do not comment on style. Facts only.

[PASTE SCRIPT BELOW]
```

## AFTER IT RUNS — your 3-minute job

1. **Open the three URLs from Pass C.** Do not read them. Just confirm the page loads and the
   title matches what the model said. A dead link or a mismatched title means it invented the
   citation — throw out the whole fact-check and run it again in a different model.
2. **Apply the fix list.** It is written as paste-ready replacements, so this is mechanical.
3. **Delete everything on the cut list.** An unsourced claim is not worth the comment it earns.
4. **Check the CONFIRMED ratio.** Below about 70% confirmed means the script was built on
   assumption, not research. Send it back.
